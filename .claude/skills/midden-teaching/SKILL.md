# Teaching midden

## What this is for

midden-interpretation guarantees an output is *explained*. This skill makes the explanation
*teach*. The user is technical, curious, and not an archaeologist or a geomorphologist. They
will read a long answer if every paragraph earns its place, and they will remember the
answer if each fact is tied to a human choice.

## The shape of a good answer

**Take the sentence apart, phrase by phrase.** When asked to explain a passage, quote each
loaded phrase in bold and explain that phrase before moving on. Do not summarise the whole
passage and hope. The reader asked because individual words were opaque; answer at the
level of the word.

**Balance: 40% earth science, 60% people.** Geology and hydrology appear only where they
explain a decision someone made: where to sleep, where to bury, where to knap, where to
carry the canoe. The test for every earth-science sentence is "so what did a person do
about that?" If there is no answer, cut it or shorten it.

**Every landform gets a human reading.** Not "this is a T1 terrace" but "this is where
people slept, cooked, and later gardened, because it is flat, dry, close to water and above
the annual flood." Not "35 m of relief" but "nobody camped up there, but it is an overlook,
a burial place, and if the rock is chert, a quarry." Terrace height sorts *kind* of site,
not site versus no site.

**Distinguish what the score can and cannot express.** The suitability surface ranks
places to live. Travel nodes (portages, fords, confluences), rock-art landmarks, quarries,
shelters under bluffs, and cemeteries on terrace edges are real archaeology that a
low-scoring cell may hold. Say so explicitly when a phrase touches one.

**Correct yourself in public.** If an earlier sentence carried an unverified claim, say so
and fix it in the teaching answer. A teaching answer that quietly repeats an error is worse
than one that visibly repairs it. (Worked example: "Ordovician limestone" at the Narrows
was a shorthand from the province label; the SGMC map says Silurian. See the corridor
reference.)

**Name periods with dates and with what they leave.** "Woodland" means nothing on its own.
"Woodland, roughly 3,000 to 1,000 years ago: pottery, the first gardens of chenopod and
sumpweed, the first built burial mounds" does.

**Commit.** Use the four uncertainty categories from midden-interpretation by name when
an anomaly comes up. In teaching prose, hedges read as evasions; a stated best guess with
its reasoning reads as honesty.

## Tone

Plain, declarative, warm. Concrete numbers with units. No bullet lists in the teaching
body: the reader is following an argument, and a list breaks it into unrelated facts. One
analogy per concept at most, and only if it is exact ("a fortress the river half-built",
"buying a 16-foot drop for 290 feet of digging").

## Sources

Draw on outside sources freely and cite them at the end. Prefer the primary and regional
literature in `references/sources.md` over general encyclopaedias, and add to that file
whenever a new source is used so the next session inherits it.
